# PaperTrail research protocol

## Query lattice

For a deep-research request, derive focused queries across these axes:

1. Problem: the target task, failure, or scientific question.
2. Mechanism: the proposed causal or computational mechanism and close synonyms.
3. Method: model families, algorithms, training regimes, measurements, and baselines.
4. Evaluation: datasets, benchmarks, metrics, populations, and operating conditions.
5. Counterevidence: negative results, limitations, confounders, failure modes, and alternative explanations.
6. Adjacencies: older terminology and neighboring fields that may contain equivalent ideas.

Start with catalog queries for recall. Use promising terminology to search exact full-text passages. Record queries that returned no evidence as well as queries that succeeded.

## Evidence ladder

Use the strongest available level and label it:

1. Exact passage: stable evidence ID from acquired full text.
2. Visual evidence: rendered page plus caption and nearby evidence IDs, visually inspected.
3. Evidence-bound structured record: model extraction whose source evidence has been checked.
4. Metadata: title and abstract only; suitable for candidate discovery, not detailed conclusions.
5. System synthesis: a new comparison or hypothesis inferred across multiple sources.

Never silently promote a lower level into a higher one.

## Novelty and opportunity tests

For each candidate idea, write:

- precise hypothesis and proposed mechanism;
- nearest indexed prior work and overlap dimensions;
- differentiating dimensions that are testable rather than cosmetic;
- strongest counterevidence or ordinary explanation;
- one experiment whose outcome could falsify the hypothesis;
- coverage boundary, including catalog-only relevant papers;
- confidence and what new evidence would change it.

An absence of search results is not evidence of global novelty. Reformulate the query on every lattice axis before reporting a bounded negative search.

## Recommended answer shape

1. Direct answer or research landscape.
2. Prior-work clusters with exact evidence references.
3. Agreements, contradictions, and unresolved assumptions.
4. Candidate gaps or ideas, each with a falsifying test.
5. Coverage and search ledger: corpus counts, date/category filters, query families, catalog-only leads, and known missing evidence.

Use compact tables only when comparing several papers or hypotheses on the same dimensions.
